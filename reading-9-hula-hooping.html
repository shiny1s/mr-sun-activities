Hula-hooping Stories

Red Group

Maya's Hula-hoop

Look at Maya.

She has a hula-hoop.

The hula-hoop is pink.

Maya spins it round and round.

She smiles and laughs.

Maya is having fun!

Think About the Story

1. How is Maya Feeling? The story says Maya smiles and laughs. How do you think she feels?

2. What Can You Tell? Maya spins the hula-hoop round and round. Why do you think she keeps spinning it?

Yellow Group

Learning to Hula-hoop

Maya wanted to learn hula-hooping. At first, the hoop kept falling down. She felt sad and didn't want to try anymore. But her friend Jake said, "Keep going! I'll help you." So Maya tried again. She moved her hips slowly and held the hoop higher. Then it worked! The hoop spun around her waist. Maya felt proud of herself.

What Can You Infer?

1. Why Did Maya Feel Better? At first Maya felt sad. Then Jake helped her and she tried again. What does this tell you about having a good friend?

2. What Made It Work? Maya moved her hips slowly and held the hoop higher. Why do you think these actions helped her succeed?

Green Group

The Hula-hoop Challenge

Maya had been practising hula-hooping every day for two weeks. Her muscles felt stronger and her movements were smooth. At the park, her teacher announced a hula-hooping contest. Maya's hands were shaking a little, but she stepped forward to participate. She kept her body still and moved only her hips in a steady rhythm. The hoop didn't wobble or fall. When she finished, her classmates cheered loudly.

Reading Between the Lines

1. Why Were Maya's Hands Shaking? The story says Maya's hands shook before the contest. What does this suggest about how she felt? What clue tells you this?

2. Practice Makes Perfect: Maya had practised every day for two weeks and her muscles felt stronger. How did her training help her during the contest?

3. Why Did Her Classmates Cheer? What can you infer about Maya's performance based on why her classmates reacted this way?

Blue Group

More Than Just a Game

Maya discovered that hula-hooping was much more than a fun playground activity. Through regular practice, she learned patience and determination. When she struggled at first, she could have given up, but she chose to persist. This decision helped her build confidence in other areas of her life too.

The hula-hoop became a symbol of her growth. Whenever Maya felt worried about trying something new, she remembered how she had mastered the hoop through hard work. She realised that many skills require practice and that failure is part of learning. This understanding changed how she approached challenges.

Deeper Thinking

1. Why Was the Hula-hoop Important? The text says the hula-hoop became "a symbol of her growth." What does this suggest about what hula-hooping meant to Maya beyond just the activity itself?

2. Making Connections: The text mentions that Maya's learning about persistence helped her "in other areas of her life too." What does this infer about how skills and attitudes can transfer between different activities?

3. What Changed? Based on the passage, infer how Maya's attitude towards challenges probably changed. What evidence from the text supports your inference?

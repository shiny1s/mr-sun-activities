Things We Care About

Everyone has something they care about. These things are called treasured items. They might not cost a lot of money. Sometimes, a treasured item is an old teddy bear. It might be a shiny shell from a fun holiday.

My favourite treasured item is a small, wooden train. My grandpa gave it to me when I was five. The paint is chipping off, but I still love it. When I hold it, I remember my grandpa's smile. I keep it safe on a high shelf in my bedroom.

Green Group

Priceless Possessions

A treasured item is a personal possession that holds special meaning to its owner. These items are often linked to happy memories, beloved family members, or important events in our lives. A treasure doesn't need to be made of gold or silver to be valuable. Its true worth comes from the feelings it gives us.

For example, my friend Sarah has a scrapbook filled with movie tickets, pressed flowers, and photographs. To anyone else, it might look like a book of scraps. But to Sarah, it is a priceless collection of her favourite days. She looks through it whenever she feels sad, and it instantly brings her comfort. We all have unique things that bring us joy.

Questions

Literal: What items does Sarah keep in her scrapbook?

Inferential: Why does the author say a treasure doesn't need to be made of gold?

Blue Group

The Value of Memories

As we grow up, we collect things that become very important to us. These are called treasured items. They are special because they remind us of past adventures, our proudest moments, and the people we love. A treasured item might not cost a lot of money in a shop. Instead, its true value is measured by the feelings it gives us. This means its value is subjective; it depends on who owns it.

Think about an old, faded photograph. To a stranger, it just shows people they do not know. But to the owner, that photo might show a wonderful family holiday or a happy childhood memory. The picture captures a special moment in time.

Another example is a piece of jewellery that is passed down through a family. This is called a family heirloom. It might be a silver necklace or an old watch. The metal might be tarnished and dull, but its history makes it incredibly special.

Treasured items anchor us to our identity and remind us of our journey. Whether it is an old diary, a handmade blanket, or a shiny stone, these objects tell the story of our lives.

Inferential: Why is the value of a treasured item described as "subjective"?

Yellow Group

Home in the Den

Wandi was a little dingo cub. He lived in a cold, snowy den with his family. The snow fell heavy and solid above them, but Wandi was not cold.

His mother had a very soft, fluffy tail. She curled it right over his body like a warm blanket. Wandi could hear his mother's heartbeat. It was constant and strong. The steady thud - thud told Wandi that he was loved and safe.

Word Power & Comprehension

1. Word Hunt: Find the word constant in the text. If his mother's heartbeat is constant, does it mean it keeps stopping, or it keeps going?

It keeps stopping and starting
It keeps going and never stops

2. Think and Write: What did Wandi use as a portable blanket to keep his nose warm?

3. Inference: How did Wandi feel when he listened to his mum's heartbeat? What clue in the story tells you that?

Green Group

The Alpine Dingo

Dingoes are native Australian animals, and they are highly adaptable creatures. There are different types of dingoes living across Australia, which are called eco-types. Wandi is an alpine dingo. Alpine dingoes mostly live in the high, cold mountains of eastern Australia.

They have very thick coats that grow fluffy in winter, and they use their big tails to shield their faces from the heavy snow. Dingoes are important apex predators. They hunt pests like rabbits and feral cats, but they are currently classed as a vulnerable species because purebred dingoes are becoming rare.

Dingo Detective Challenges

1. Vocabulary Check: Look at the phrase apex predator. Based on the text, what does an apex predator do?

2. Fact Retrieval: What is an "eco-type"? Which specific eco-type is Wandi?

3. Deep Thinking: The text says dingoes are "vulnerable". Using clues from the passage, why do you think humans need to protect them from becoming extinct?

Blue Group

Authorship and the Guardian of the Ecosystem

When Favel Parrett wrote the book Wandi, she wanted to change our perspective on wild dingoes. For over 200 years, dingoes have been widely misunderstood because they look like domestic dogs, yet they are actually crucial to the Australian ecosystem.

As an apex predator, the dingo acts as an environmental guardian by keeping invasive pests in check and preventing kangaroo overpopulation. Favel Parrett has immense authority on this subject matter. Before writing, she worked directly as a volunteer at the Dingo Discovery Sanctuary. Because she has real-world experience observing alpine dingo eco-types, readers trust her authorship and find her arguments highly credible. We must listen to her message so we can preserve these unique creatures for future generations.

Author's Craft & Analysis

1. Vocabulary Mastery: Define authority and authorship in your own words based on how they are used in the text.

2. Language Feature Hunt: Locate and copy down one declarative sentence from the text that states a clear fact about dingoes.

3. Evaluative Critique: What was Favel Parrett's primary purpose in writing Wandi? Do you think sharing a story from a single dingo's perspective is an effective way to persuade people to protect them? Explain your viewpoint.

Reading Groups

Red Group

Sam's Smooth Stone

Very simple text for early readers. Red now tells one clear story about Sam and her special stone.

Sam has a smooth stone. It is round and grey.

She found it with her dad at the river.

Sam keeps the stone in a small red bag.

The stone is her treasured item. It helps Sam think of Pop.

She likes to hold it when she feels sad.

Questions

1. What is Sam's treasured item?

2. Where did Sam find the stone?

3. What colour is the bag?

4. Who was with Sam?

5. Why does Sam like to hold the stone?

Sort and say

Say what happened first, next, and last. Then write it in your book.

Yellow Group

Nan's Button Tin

Nan has a round tin on her shelf. The tin is old, but she keeps it clean and dry.

Inside the tin are many buttons. Some are shiny. Some are small. Some are made of wood.

Nan says the buttons are special because they came from family clothes long ago. When she opens the tin, she tells stories about the people who wore them.

The tin is a treasured item because it helps Nan remember happy times.

Questions

1. Where does Nan keep the tin?

2. Why are the buttons special?

3. Which word best describes some of the buttons?

Write in your book

Copy the sentence that tells why the tin is treasured.

Short response

Write one sentence: What treasured item do you have at home?

Green Group

The Old Camera

My uncle keeps an old camera in a soft black case. It does not work any more, but he still looks after it carefully.

The camera once belonged to his dad. He used it to take photos at birthdays, school concerts, and family trips. Some of the pictures are a little blurry, yet they still show real smiles and special moments.

My uncle says the camera is precious because it reminds him of his father. It also reminds him that ordinary days can become important memories later on.

Even though phones are easier to use now, he would never throw the camera away.

Questions

1. What does precious mean in this text?

2. Why does the uncle keep the camera?

3. What do the old photos still show?

Write in your book

Copy one sentence that shows why the camera matters to the uncle.

Thinking task

Why can an old object still be important, even if it no longer works?

Blue Group

Asha's Grandfather's Watch

Asha keeps her grandfather's pocket watch in a small wooden drawer beside her bed. The watch no longer tells the right time, and its silver case is scratched from many years of use.

Even so, Asha treats it carefully. Her grandfather carried the watch when he travelled to Australia as a young man. He later gave it to Asha and told her that it reminded him to use his time wisely and to stay hopeful during difficult seasons.

Whenever Asha opens the watch, she sees his neat initials inside the lid. That tiny detail makes the object feel personal and real, as if part of his story is still close to her. The watch is not worth much money, but it has deep emotional value because it links Asha to her family history.

Asha also thinks the watch teaches her something important. It reminds her that treasured items can carry lessons, not just memories. For her, the watch represents courage, movement, and the choices people make when beginning a new life.

Questions

1. Where does Asha keep the watch?

2. Why is the watch special to Asha?

3. What does the word emotional most nearly mean in this text?

4. What do the initials inside the lid show?

5. Which idea is a lesson Asha learns from the watch?

Write in your book

Copy one sentence that shows why the watch has emotional value.

Extended response

Explain how the watch represents both memory and lesson. Use evidence from the story.

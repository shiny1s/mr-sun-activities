🖨️ 3D Prizes

Learning Gratitude - Reading Groups

Red Group

Saying Thank You

Mr. Sun has a 3D printer in his classroom. He prints small, fun toys to give as prizes when students do good work. At first, everyone was very happy. But now, some students complain. They want bigger toys. They want different colours. They do not say thank you anymore.

A prize is a gift, not something you must get. Mr. Sun feels sad when students are ungrateful. We should always show appreciation for hard work.

Comprehension Questions

1. Vocabulary: What does ungrateful mean?

2. Finding Evidence: Which sentence in the text shows how Mr. Sun feels?

3. Literal: What does Mr. Sun use the 3D printer for?

4. Making Connections: How is receiving a prize from Mr. Sun like getting a birthday present?

5. Vocabulary: What is a prize?

6. Inference: Why did the students stop saying thank you?

Yellow Group

The 3D Printing Club

Every Friday, Mr. Sun gives out 3D printed prizes for good behaviour. The printer hums and buzzes all week to make them. Lately, a problem has started. Some students are acting entitled. This means they think they deserve a big reward without earning it.

If Mr. Sun gives a student a blue prize, they complain that it is not gold. They forget that the printer takes hours to make just one prize! Students need to remember that these rewards are a bonus. They are not owed to anyone. We should practice being thankful and recognise the effort Mr. Sun puts in.

Comprehension Questions

1. Vocabulary: What does entitled mean?

2. Finding Evidence: What detail from the text proves that making prizes takes a long time?

3. Making Connections: If someone gives you a bonus, how should you react?

4. Literal: When does Mr. Sun give out the prizes?

5. Vocabulary: What does effort mean?

6. Inference: What might Mr. Sun do if the students keep complaining?

Green Group

Understanding the Effort Behind the Prize

In Mr. Sun's class, the 3D printer is used to reward students for their hard work and achievements. However, Mr. Sun has noticed a worrying trend. Some students are showing less gratitude and are becoming more demanding about their printed prizes.

Many students ask for large, detailed models, such as articulated dragons, without realising that these can take more than 20 hours to print and use a lot of plastic. When they receive a smaller prize instead, they often complain or show disappointment. They do not always understand the cost of the filament or the time needed to run and maintain the 3D printers. It is important that students learn to appreciate the effort and thought behind these rewards, rather than expecting them as something they automatically deserve.

Comprehension Questions

1. Vocabulary: What does gratitude mean?

2. Finding Evidence: Which part of the text explains why making large models is difficult?

3. Vocabulary: What is filament?

4. Literal: What do students often do when they receive a smaller prize?

5. Making Connections: How is receiving a printed prize similar to someone cooking a meal for you?

6. Inference: Why is Mr. Sun worried about his students' behaviour?

Blue Group

Expecting Too Much

Mr. Sun loves using his 3D printer to make learning fun. He often prints small, clever puzzles and tokens to reward students for their hard work. At the beginning of the year, a simple 3D-printed bookmark was seen as an amazing prize. Every student was thrilled to receive one.

However, as the months passed, Mr. Sun noticed a big change. We are now seeing a lack of gratitude. Students frequently complain about the size, colour, and quality of their rewards. They demand complex, multi-coloured dragons or large action figures. They do not think about the high cost of the plastic filament that Mr. Sun buys with his own money.

They also do not realise how long these items take to print. A large model can tie up the printer for an entire weekend! This shift in behaviour is very sad. Students are acting entitled, treating the classroom like a free toy store.

They forget that Mr. Sun spends his own personal time after school fixing the printer and making sure it works, just to give them fun prizes. It is very important that we change this attitude. Students must learn to show genuine appreciation for the extra effort their teachers make. A small reward is a symbol of a job well done, not a product to complain about.

Comprehension Questions

1. Vocabulary: What does entitled mean?

2. Finding Evidence: What evidence in the text shows that Mr. Sun is using his own personal resources?

3. Making Connections: How is the students' behaviour like treating the classroom like a "free toy store"?

4. Literal: What did students think of the 3D-printed bookmarks at the beginning of the year?

5. Vocabulary: What does appreciation mean?

6. Inference: Why did the students' feelings about the prizes change over the months?
